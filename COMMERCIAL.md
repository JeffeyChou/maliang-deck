# Commercial Licensing

Maliang Deck is open source under AGPL-3.0-or-later. Commercial licenses are
available for teams who want to build with Maliang Deck but need terms that fit
their product, company policy, or customer commitments.

This document is not itself a commercial license. It describes the intended
commercial licensing paths so conversations can start from a shared
understanding. Final commercial terms are handled in a separate signed
agreement.

## When A Commercial License Helps

The AGPL route is a good fit for open collaboration and source-sharing
workflows. A commercial license may be a better fit if you need:

- proprietary modifications;
- private redistribution to customers or partners;
- hosted SaaS or network-service use under custom terms;
- OEM, white-label, or embedded distribution;
- enterprise internal deployment under custom legal terms;
- paid support, custom feature development, or SLA-backed maintenance;
- private templates, brand systems, workflow integrations, or exporter work.

## Intended License Tiers

### SaaS License

For hosted products that use Maliang Deck to generate or edit presentations,
including token-based or subscription-based PPT generation services.

Typical terms to define in the final agreement:

- allowed hosted product and domains;
- user or usage limits;
- source and modification obligations;
- attribution and brand-use rules;
- support level and update access;
- fee model.

### Enterprise License

For internal company deployment, private workflows, custom templates, private
document systems, and business use that benefits from custom terms.

Typical terms to define in the final agreement:

- named organization and affiliates;
- internal-use scope;
- seat, server, or usage limits;
- private modification rights;
- support and maintenance;
- security and confidentiality terms.

### OEM / Redistribution License

For teams that want to embed Maliang Deck into another product, plugin,
platform, or redistributed package.

Typical terms to define in the final agreement:

- product and distribution channel;
- redistribution rights;
- sublicensing limits;
- attribution and brand-use rules;
- update and security obligations;
- royalty or flat-fee model.
